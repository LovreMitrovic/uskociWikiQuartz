Попис становништва и земље у околини Задра из 1756. године

Sep 08, 20265 min read

Popis stanovništva i zemlje u okolini Zadra iz 1756. godine

Summary

A Venetian land-and-souls survey of the Zadar contado, carried out under the Grimani agrarian law of 1756 (legge Grimani), published by Gligor Stanojević from the original in the Venetian state archive. It enumerates, village by village, every household head by name, the number of anime (souls) in the household, and the household’s landholding in campi / quarte / tavole, split into land held con laudemio and senza laudemio, with an in tutto total.

24 villages of the Zadar district are covered, from the coastal strip (Sukošan, Turanj, Sv. Filip i Jakov, Pakoštane, Biograd) through Ravni kotari and into Bukovica (Nadin, Korlat, Rastević, Biljane, Pristeg). Zemunik is enumerated as two separate communities — the villici (Croatian peasant community) and the Albanesi, the Albanian colony settled from the Bar / Šestani region in 1726–1733.

Only heads of household are named; all other household members appear as a soul count. There is therefore no kinship data (no spouse, child, or parent names) beyond occasional quondam patronymics naming a deceased father. Alongside the resident heads the survey lists non-resident landholders — Zadar patrician and citizen families holding land in these villages (Rotta, Stratico, Filippi, Adobati, Albinoni, Bonicelli, Vlasto, Detrico, Canagetti, Bianchi, Piovesana, Costantini, Diana, Giusti, Soppe, Benja, Fanfogna) — and institutional entries (chiesa, paroco, carica di capitanio).

Citation

Глигор Станојевић, “Попис становништва и земље у околини Задра из 1756. године”, Мешовита грађа (Miscellanea), књ. XVI (1987), Историјски институт, Београд; Просвета, Београд 1988, стр. 49–126. Original: Archivio di Stato di Venezia, Provveditori generali in Dalmazia et Albania, filza 596.

Reliability and biases

  • Primary in content (a contemporary official fiscal-agrarian enumeration), delivered here as a modern scholarly transcription — high reliability for each household head’s name, village, household size and landholding in 1756.
  • Names are recorded in Venetian chancery orthography (Chiurcovich = Ćurković, Xorich / Zorich = Zorić, Doglianaz = Doljanac). Croatized forms in this wiki are editorial normalizations; where the normalization is uncertain the Person page carries confidence: low, and the original spelling is always preserved in given_name_variants / surname_variants and in the body prose.
  • The tracked artifact in raw/ is an OCR/vision transcription, not a text-layer extraction: the PDF is a scan with no text layer (pdftotext returns nothing). Figures should be checked against the original scan before being used in quantitative analysis.
  • The introductory essay (pp. 49–51) is not present in the transcription and was deliberately not OCR’d (user decision, 2026-09-08). The file carries only a placeholder summarising it. Stanojević’s own methodology notes, his village count, and his demographic commentary are therefore not available to this wiki, and none of the interpretation here rests on them.
  • Page-order defects, acknowledged by Stanojević himself (“странице текста нијесу уредно сложене”): pp. 60–63, 66–69 and parts of pp. 96–101 contain duplicated or re-sorted entries. Two consequences were handled during ingest:
    • Donje Jagodnje (pp. 85–87) opens with a garbled block of 9 households (Plazibat, Vrtača, two Žepina, three Žarković, Pantalija, Kovačević) repeating entries that recur later with full land figures. Merged to the fuller entry; the duplication is noted on each Person page. For Toma Žepina (19 vs 10 souls) and Miše Žepina (26 vs 22) the two copies disagree on the soul count — both figures are recorded in the page Notes.
    • Biograd (pp. 64–69) is split across two headings that are largely complementary alphabetical runs; Zuanne Rudich and Marco Rudich appear in both and were merged. Treated throughout as one village.
  • Multi-digit tavole values are frequently faint in the scan; these are the least reliable figures in the source.

Key facts extracted

  • Villages: 24 (Zemunik counted once, enumerated as two communities).
  • Table rows: 1,504 — of which 1,317 resident household heads, 114 named non-resident landholders, 64 institutional entries (chiesa, paroco, carica di capitanio), and 12 transcription duplicates merged away.
  • Souls enumerated: 10,330 across the 1,317 resident households (mean household ≈ 7.8).
  • Persons added to wiki from this source: 1,431 (see log entry 2026-09-08).
  • Surnames: 681 canonical — 553 new hubs, 128 existing hubs gained 1756 bearers.
  • Places: 24 village rosters — 8 new Place pages, 16 existing.

Relation to other sources

Five villages carry rosters from both this survey and the Dundovic-1610-Zadar-Census — Sukošan, Turanj, Sv. Filip i Jakov, Pakoštane, Biograd na Moru. Each of those Place pages now has a ## 1610 → 1756: what changed section comparing household counts, souls and surname turnover across the 146-year gap, with the likely same-family pairs that the two ingests’ differing normalizations split apart.

Surname hubs carrying bearers from both censuses (e.g. Smolić, Bilić, Rudić, Pavić, Letina, Gracalić, Matošić, Krečić, Raspović, Zorić) show the two rosters as separate sections.

Backfilled 2026-09-08: Sv. Filip i Jakov is in the 1610 census — 16 heads, 102 souls — but the 2026-07-21 Dundović ingest had skipped it. Those 16 households were added to the wiki and the village now has a comparison section like the other four.
