Osmanski Vlasi Istre na izvornoj Tromeđi: nestabilna podaništva i ambivalentne lojalnosti na imperijalnom višegraničju u Hrvatskoj 16. stoljeća

Jan 01, 20243 min read

Šarić 2024 — Osmanski Vlasi Istre na izvornoj Tromeđi

Summary

Scholarly analysis of the “Vlasi Istre” (Ottoman: Eflâkân-i Istryia; Venetian: Morlachi istriani) — a 16th-c. Vlach group that fled Croatian lands ahead of Ottoman conquest c. 1518–1523, settled in Habsburg NE Istria and Kras, then returned in 1528–1530 to the Klis Sanjak under Ottoman istimâlet (winning-over) policy. The article traces their reversible cyclical migrations across Ottoman, Venetian, and Habsburg territories until they disappear as a recognizable group c. 1585.

Citation

Šarić, Marko. “Osmanski Vlasi Istre na izvornoj Tromeđi: nestabilna podaništva i ambivalentne lojalnosti na imperijalnom višegraničju u Hrvatskoj 16. stoljeća.” Povijesni prilozi 67 (2024): 179–236. DOI: 10.22586/pp.v43i67.30587.

Reliability and biases

  • Form: peer-reviewed scholarly article (izvorni znanstveni rad).
  • Author: Marko Šarić, Department of History, Faculty of Humanities and Social Sciences, University of Zagreb. Specialist in 16th–17th c. Vlach/Morlach studies and the Triplex Confinium.
  • Source type: secondary. The article analyzes but does not fully transcribe the underlying primary registers. Claims drawn from registers should cite “Šarić 2024” with the underlying register noted in the body, not as if the register were directly read.
  • Strengths: extensive use of Ottoman tax registers (defters) cross-referenced with Venetian state correspondence and Habsburg records. Engages with prior literature (Buzov 1990, Juran 2022, Roksandić 2003, Caciur 2015–16, Bracewell 1997).
  • Limitations for genealogy:
    • Most register-only individuals appear with patronymic only (e.g. “Petar, sin Milovana”) — fixed surnames had not yet stabilized in 16th c.
    • Many village locations marked “nepoznato” (unknown) — toponymic gaps from later depopulation and resettlement.
    • Confessional identity (Catholic / Orthodox) is hard to determine from given names alone in this period.
    • Ottoman registers list household heads; women, children, and dependents are aggregated.

Key concepts introduced

  • istimâlet — Ottoman strategy of winning over local populations through privileges, exemptions, and accommodations rather than coercion.
  • Tromeđa / Triplex Confinium — the original tripoint between Ottoman, Venetian, Habsburg territories, focused around Novigradsko more, Karinsko more, and the Velebit channel.
  • dzemat / katun — Vlach community/lineage unit, often headed by a knez or vojvoda and primićur.
  • filurija — Vlach household tax (replacing other dues).
  • timar — Ottoman benefice; granted to some Vlach starešine.
  • Morlaci Istrijani — Venetian-side counterpart to the Ottoman Vlasi Istre, on the Zadar contado from c. 1540.
  • Ćići / Ćiribirci — bilingual (Slavic + Balkan-Romance) Vlachs, last attested speakers in Istria/Kras (modern Istro-Romanian).

Notes for the wiki

  • This source is the spine of wiki coverage for 16th-c. Vlach migrations in the original Tromeđa.
  • When citing a register-attested fact, record the register year (1528–30 / 1540 / 1550 / 1574 / 1585) in the body, then cite this article: [[Saric-2024-Vlasi-Istre]].
  • The label “Vlasi Istre” is exogenous — an Ottoman administrative classification, not a self-identifier.
  • For each register-only person where surname is uncertain, the patronymic stem (e.g. “Milovan” → “Milovanić”) may have stabilized as a surname later; treat as the canonical surname when the article confirms continuity, otherwise file under the patronymic-derived form.
