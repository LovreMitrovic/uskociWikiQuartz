Biograd na Moru

Coastal town of northern Dalmatia south of Zadar, called Zaravecchia (Zara Vecchia) in Venetian records. Venetian commune and pertinenza HQ from the 1689 Cornaro Liga (with Krčina, Toretta, Sv. Filip i Jakov, Pakoštane); Mattio Žabetić captain; site of 1704 unrest Desnica-1951-Kotarski-Uskoci-II. Home of the Biograd kapetani in Venetian service from the late 16th c. — e.g. Juraj-Matkovic-b????-Biograd (1581) Saric-2024-Vlasi-Istre. Among the places where the Spingaroli were attested. With Pakoštane plotted Šimun Bortulačić’s murder 1692; pardoned 1694.

Notes

Venetian Zaravecchia / Zara Vecchia = modern Biograd na Moru (merged from former separate pages Biograd, Zaravecchia). Distinct from Stari Grad (a separate locality near Biograd, in the Mitrović sub-region) and from Starigrad (Starigrad-Paklenica).

Biograd grows from 80 households to 132 and from 431 souls to 820, but only 8 of 51 surnames survive the gap — the lowest continuity rate of the four. Tomić expands from 2 households to 6 and Bilić from 1 to 3; Veličić and Lukačić shrink. Likely the same family, split by spelling

  • 1610 Jeličić (2, Gelicich) ~ 1756 Jelčić (3, Gelicich) — The two ingests normalized the identical Venetian string Gelicich differently — Jeličić in 1610, Jelčić in 1756. Same name, same town; a normalization artifact, not a population change.
  • 1610 Pilizarić (4, Pilizarich) ~ 1756 Pelizarić (3, Pelizarich)
  • 1610 Dabanović (1, Dabanouich) ~ 1756 Dobanović (3, Dobanovich)
  • 1610 Zmajčić (1, Smaicich) ~ 1756 Smajčić (1, Smaicich) — Again the identical Venetian string Smaicich, normalized two ways.
  • 1610 Maratov (1, Maratou) ~ 1756 Marat (1, Marat)

These are flagged, not merged — merging Person or Surname pages needs a decision.

A surname that became a byname

Vitanović is one of the larger Biograd families in 1610 — 5 households. It is absent from the 1756 surname list, but it survives in the survey as a byname: Toma Mersun detto Vitanovich and Sime Mersun detto Vitanovich in Biograd, Toma Mersum detto Vitanovich in Vrana, and Misco Zorich detto Vitanovich in Biograd Stanojevic-1756-Zadar-Census. The line did not disappear; it was absorbed into the Mršun and Zorić households and demoted to a distinguishing epithet. This is the one demonstrable 1610→1756 family link in the village, and it is invisible if you compare surname lists alone.

How much of this turnover is real?

Some of it is certainly an artifact. The two censuses were written by different scribes 146 years apart, and their Venetian spellings were normalized into Croatian by two separate ingests using different judgement — so one family can enter the wiki under two hubs and read as a disappearance plus an arrival. The pairs listed above are the visible cases; there are probably more that no longer look similar enough to spot. Treat the continuity figures as a lower bound.

The rest is real, and has a cause: the Morean War (1684–1699) and the Venetian resettlement that followed it moved large numbers of people into the Zadar contado from Ottoman territory. Both effects push in the same direction, which is why the raw overlap is so low.
