# Krmpotić (Krmpote / Carapotani)

Catholic Vlach (Bunjevac) lineage of western Hercegovinian origin — one of the **three core Bunjevac katuns** (with [[Vojnic]] and [[Sladovic]]) named in the 1477 Ottoman defter. The article calls them "izrazito vitalna i oportuna vlaška grupacija" — exceptionally vital and adaptive — and a "sociodemografska paradigma tromeđe" for their reversible cyclical migrations across all three imperial frontiers [[Saric-2008-Bunjevci]].

## 1477 attestation
Hercegovina defter, **nahija Humska zemlja**: džemat of [[Vukic-Krmpotic-b????-Hercegovina]], 11 houses (poreznih jedinica). The defter notes they had returned from refuge: "od ranije su to bile njihove baštine pa su ponovo došli pod tim uvjetom." Winter pastures (zimišta) in [[Buhovo]], [[Borajna]], [[Ledinac]], [[Veselivstak]] (W. Hercegovina, 22–35 km from river Buna). Summer pastures (ljetišta) on [[Cvrsnica]], [[Vran]], [[Ljubusa]] [[Saric-2008-Bunjevci]].

## Migration trajectory (Civran 1615 + Šarić reconstruction)
- **c. 1528** — moved into Lika, settling around [[Gracac]]; possibly attested in the stećak of Ivan Balenović in Kijani (knez Mihovil, 1528–30 popis ličko-pounskoga krajišta).
- **post-1573** — after Cyprus War, settled by Ottomans in zaleđe Karina (nahija Obrovac); their village named [[Medvidja]] "Krmpote" (today Staro Selo, with a *bunar žive vode* called Krmpot). Mletačke izvore: *Morlacchi Caranpotani*.
- **1605** — first migration to Habsburg territory: 24+ named families settle [[Lic]] on Zrinski estate of [[Hreljin]]. Surnames: Balinović (Balen), Barulović, Božić, Budisalić, Butorčić ([[Butorac]]), Cvitić, Draganović, Hromić, Kovač, Krivalica, Krmpotić, Krnjac, Malovridnjak, Matijević, Marković, Mihovilović, Mikulić, Miletić, Oproković, Pećanić, Petrović, Skorupović (Skorup), Šimunović, Vukoslavić.
- **1615** — kapetan gulfa [[Andrea-Civran-b????-Venice]] records their oral tradition in negotiations to relocate to Venetian Istria.
- **1627** — second wave to Lič: Blažević, Deranja, Filipović, Jovanović, Krpan, Pavelić, Pavličević, Peričić, Prpić, Radošević, [[Starcevic]], Šojat, Tomić, Tomičić, Tomljanović, Vukelić (Pavličević 1989 reconstruction; medium confidence).
- **1636** — Forza vizitacija: župa Bukovica (Krmpote, Popovići, Ervenik, Parčić, Žužule), 1180 souls under fra Ivan Visovački; ~400 in selo Krmpote alone.
- **1647** (Candian War) — harambaša [[Harambasa-Njegovan-b????-Medvidja]] led 40–50 Krmpote families from Medviđa to [[Vinjerac]] and [[Starigrad]]; some onward to [[Pag]].
- **1605–1690** — settled also [[Krmpote-Senj]] (Sv. Jakov), [[Krivi-Put]], [[Ledenice]], [[Senjska-Draga]], [[Radovin]], [[Poljica-Zadarska]], [[Vinjerac]]; some to [[Mletacka-Istra]].
- **1690+** — major participation in Lika–Krbava recolonization, especially [[Pazariste]], [[Smiljan]], [[Lovinac]].

## Confessional note
Mixed-confession at start of 17th c. (some Orthodox families joined the Lič migration); by ~1700 fully Catholic. The article cites Sladović 1856, Roksandić 1982, Pavličević 1989 on this multiconfessional aspect [[Saric-2008-Bunjevci]].

## In Desnica II (1684–1749)

- **Luka Njegovan** — brought 40-50 Krmpote families from Bukovica to **Vinjerac** during the Candian War (doc. 228 / Velebit Podgorje context) [[Desnica-1951-Kotarski-Uskoci-II]].
- **harambaša Mede Milcović (Mede Milicovich)** — "capo di Crampote" (head of Bukovica-Krmpote villagers near Zelengrad); settled Krmpote and Parčić villages in Bukovica (15 houses each); granted **50 fields at Medvidja near church 1693-01-21**; later (1697) banned from harassing Parčić Morlacchi (docs. 263, 301) [[Desnica-1951-Kotarski-Uskoci-II]].

### Krmpote disambiguation
The Bukovica Krmpote settlement of Mede Milcović (near Zelengrad) is a **third** Krmpote distinct from (a) the Senj-area [[Krmpote-Senj]] and (b) the older Bukovica Krmpote at Medvidja (Staro Selo) [[Desnica-1951-Kotarski-Uskoci-II]].
