# Šarić 2008 — Bunjevci u ranome novom vijeku

## Summary
Synthesizing scholarly study of the **Bunjevci** as an early-modern ("predmoderna") ethnic community: Catholic Vlach katuns from western Hercegovina (the 1477 katuns of **Krmpote**, **Vojnići**, **Sladovići** and others), colonized by Ottoman authorities into the *vilajet Hrvati* / Klis sandjak in the early 16th c. Their identity crystallized through the **bunjevci ↔ rkaći** dichotomy with Orthodox Vlachs around mid-16th c.

The article disentangles four branches that emerge by the 17th c. — **western-Bosnian** (Ottoman), **Dalmatian** (Venetian), **Lika–Primorje** (Habsburg), and **Danubian/Bačka** (Hungarian) — and traces their reversible cyclical migrations. It establishes that the Buna-river origin legend is a 17th-c. Bosnian-Franciscan legitimating construct, and argues the etymon is the Vlach anthroponym **Bun/Bunj** (< Latin *Bonifacius* via Balkan-Romance vocalism), with patronymic suffix *-evac/-evci* — a parallel formation to medieval Vlach groups Bunčići, Bunovići, Bunuševci.

## Reliability and biases
- **Form**: scholarly synthetic article; companion piece to [[Saric-2024-Vlasi-Istre]] and same author's master's thesis (Šarić 2005, *Dinarski Vlasi…*).
- **Author**: [[Marko-Saric-b????-Zagreb]], FFZG, leading specialist on early-modern Dinaric Vlach studies.
- **Source type**: secondary, but draws on a wide spread of primary registers (Ottoman defters 1477/1554/1561, Lika–Krbava 1712, Venetian *commissiones*, Bosnian Franciscan reports).
- **Strengths**: explicitly anti-presentist methodology; rejects both Croat-only and Serb-only readings of Bunjevci as projections of modern nationality onto a pre-modern community. Comparative onomastic, lingusitic, and ethnographic argumentation.
- **Limitations for genealogy**:
  - Earliest 16th-c. attestations are at the level of Vlach group names (Krmpote, Vojnići), not individual surnames.
  - Bunjevac surnames stabilize across the 17th c.; before that bearers appear under patronymic + village.
  - Article is interpretive — for individual genealogies, the underlying primary sources (Aličić 1985 defter; Kaser 2003 Lika 1712 census; Pavličević 1989 on Krmpote migrations) are authoritative.
  - The 1605 and 1627 Lič settlement family lists come via Pavličević (compiled from later 18th-c. records and inferred for 1627); treat as plausible but not register-direct.

## Key concepts introduced
- **bunjevci ↔ rkaći** — confessional-onomastic dichotomy (Catholic vs. Orthodox Vlach), forming mid-16th c. on the Tromeđa.
- **etnokonfesionalizam** — fusion of ethnic and confessional identity in 16th-c. Vlach society, driven by Pećka patrijaršija (renewed 1557) and post-Tridentine Catholic renewal.
- **Buna-river legend** — first recorded by Fortis 1778 (Lika), Lanosović 1794 (Bačka). Šarić argues it is a Bosnian-Franciscan construct legitimating their pastoral jurisdiction over Dinaric Catholic Vlachs against Dalmatian dioceses.
- **Bun/Bunj anthroponym** — Vlach personal name from Latin *Bonifacius* via Balkan-Romance vocalic shift *o > u*; cf. medieval Bunčići, Bunovići (Tudoričevci, Dečani), Bunuševci (Vranje), Bunislavići (1406 east Hum).
- **"krajinska etnija"** — frontier-shaped ethnicity; Bunjevci formed in Ottoman *serhad* conditions and shaped by reversible cross-border migrations.
- **Meerkroaten / Catholische Walahen / Rasciani catholici** — administrative names used by Habsburg/Hungarian authorities; "Bunjevac" itself rarely used in official records before mid-18th c.
- **Senjsko-modruška biskupija** — first ecclesiastical institution to introduce "Bunjevac" into official usage (biskup Martin Brajković, 1702: *catholici Valachi alias Bunievczi*).
