# Conquest of Sinj (25–30 September 1686)

Provveditor Generale **[[Girolamo-Cornaro-b????-Venice|Girolamo Cornaro]]** took **[[Sinj]]** in a 5-day siege and assault, recovering the seven cannon abandoned in the Venetian disaster of April 1685 and reversing the most painful Venetian defeat of the Morean War to that point (doc 144, 145, [[Desnica-1951-Kotarski-Uskoci-II]]).

## Order of battle (Venetian)
- Camp at [[Solin|Salona]] under Cornaro
- General **Prince of Parma**
- General **San Polo**
- Sergente maggior di battaglia **marchese dal Borro**
- Conte **Baldassar Spolverino**, colonnello (wounded in assault)
- Tenente colonnello **Pizzamano** (later commander of Sinj garrison)
- Tenente colonnello **Rizzi** (distinguished himself)
- Tenente colonnello **Cittadella** (wounded by stone)
- Engineer **Camuccio**
- Battery superintendent **De Buccò** (originally appointed by Valier)
- Battery commanders **[[Antonio-Loredan-b????-Venice|Antonio Loredan]]** (di Zuanne) and **[[Vicenzo-Gritti-b????-Venice|Vicenzo Gritti]]** (di Alvise)
- Paymaster **Lorenzo Zorzi** (fu Antonio)
- Reinforcements from Makarska under **[[Tranquillo-Bollani-b????-Venice|Tranquillo Bollani]]**
- **[[Antonio-Bollani-b????-Venice|Antonio Bollani]]** appointed first provveditore of newly-conquered Sinj

## Casualties
- Venetian: 25–30 dead, 120+ wounded
- Ottoman: 150+ killed in the assault
- Recovered: 7 cannon

## Aftermath
The Sinj garrison under Pizzamano held off Atlagić's attempted re-siege in April 1687 (doc 169, relieved by Cornaro and [[Antonio-Zen-b????-Venice|Antonio Zen]]). Sinj remained Venetian thereafter and was a launching point for the 1687 raids into Hercegovina (Duvno, where [[Stojan-Jankovic-b1635-Zegar|Stojan Janković]] was killed).
