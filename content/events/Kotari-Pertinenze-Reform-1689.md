# Kotari Pertinenze Reform (11 March 1689)

By ordinance of **[[Girolamo-Cornaro-b????-Venice|Girolamo Cornaro]]** (doc 224, [[Desnica-1951-Kotarski-Uskoci-II]]), the territory of [[Zadar]] was divided into **9 administrative pertinenze** (units), each under a named governor and a dedicated league of judges. The reform regulated:

- **Descrizione** rules (the Venetian land-population census)
- Agricultural rights (pasturage, ploughing, cultivation of abandoned Ottoman lands)
- **Decime** (tithe) collection procedures
- Militia rolls — every village to know its capi and its mobilisation strength

The reform formalised the patchwork of *sopraintendenze* the four Morlach capi had held informally since 1684 (Posedarski, Bortulačić, Janković, Smiljanić) plus the four Mocenigo "new" capi (Soppe brothers, Ventura, Alberti). Following the deaths of Stojan Janković (1687), Smoljan Smiljanić (by 1689), and the elder Posedarski brothers, the reform was also a generational handoff — the new colonnello Frane Posedarski is identified in the ordinance as "figlio e nipote respective de medemi" (son of one, nephew of the other).

The Liga statute was reissued by **[[Daniel-Dolfin-IV-b????-Venice|Daniel Dolfin]]** on 12 February 1696 (doc later in series).

## Notes
- This is the foundational governance instrument of the Venetian Ravni Kotari for the rest of the Republic's tenure (until 1797). All later Venetian administrators worked within or amended this framework.
- The Mitrović–Sinobad and Mitrović–Milković reconciliations of 1692 (under Dolfin) operate within the pertinenze framework; the 1704 Bukovica revolt is a partial breakdown of it.
