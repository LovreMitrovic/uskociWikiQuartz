# Biograd na Moru

Coastal town of northern Dalmatia south of [[Zadar]], called **Zaravecchia** (Zara Vecchia) in Venetian records. Venetian commune and pertinenza HQ from the 1689 Cornaro Liga (with Krčina, Toretta, [[Sv-Filip-i-Jakov|Sv. Filip i Jakov]], [[Pakostane|Pakoštane]]); Mattio Žabetić captain; site of 1704 unrest [[Desnica-1951-Kotarski-Uskoci-II]]. Home of the *Biograd kapetani* in Venetian service from the late 16th c. — e.g. [[Juraj-Matkovic-b????-Biograd]] (1581) [[Saric-2024-Vlasi-Istre]]. Among the places where the [[Spingaroli]] were attested. With [[Pakostane|Pakoštane]] plotted Šimun Bortulačić's murder 1692; pardoned 1694.

## Notes
Venetian **Zaravecchia** / **Zara Vecchia** = modern Biograd na Moru (merged from former separate pages Biograd, Zaravecchia). Distinct from [[Stari-Grad-Zaravecchia|Stari Grad]] (a separate locality near Biograd, in the Mitrović sub-region) and from [[Starigrad]] (Starigrad-Paklenica).

Biograd grows from 80 households to 132 and from 431 souls to 820, but only 8 of 51 surnames survive the gap — the lowest continuity rate of the four. [[Tomic|Tomić]] expands from 2 households to 6 and [[Bilic|Bilić]] from 1 to 3; [[Velicic|Veličić]] and [[Lukacic|Lukačić]] shrink. Biograd was sacked and largely emptied during the Morean War, and the 1756 roster reads as a substantially new population.

### A surname that became a byname
[[Vitanovic|Vitanović]] is one of the larger Biograd families in 1610 — 5 households. It is absent from the 1756 surname list, but it survives in the survey as a **byname**: *Toma Mersun detto Vitanovich* and *Sime Mersun detto Vitanovich* in Biograd, *Toma Mersum detto Vitanovich* in Vrana, and *Misco Zorich detto Vitanovich* in Biograd [[Stanojevic-1756-Zadar-Census]]. The line did not disappear; it was absorbed into the [[Mrsun|Mršun]] and [[Zoric|Zorić]] households and demoted to a distinguishing epithet. This is the one demonstrable 1610→1756 family link in the village, and it is invisible if you compare surname lists alone.


### How much of this turnover is real?
Some of it is certainly an artifact. The two censuses were written by different scribes 146 years apart, and their Venetian spellings were normalized into Croatian by two separate ingests using different judgement — so one family can enter the wiki under two hubs and read as a disappearance plus an arrival. The pairs listed above are the visible cases; there are probably more that no longer look similar enough to spot. Treat the continuity figures as a **lower bound**.

The rest is real, and has a cause: the Morean War (1684–1699) and the Venetian resettlement that followed it moved large numbers of people into the Zadar contado from Ottoman territory. Both effects push in the same direction, which is why the raw overlap is so low.
